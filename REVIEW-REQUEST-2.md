## The project in one paragraph

Static website (hand-written HTML/CSS/JS, no framework, no build step at deploy, zero npm dependencies) for a Thai–Hungarian fusion restaurant in Budapest, next to Heroes' Square. Hungarian company, so GDPR and ePrivacy apply. No payments, no accounts, no database. Hungarian at `/`, English at `/en/`. Netlify hosting, GitHub CI. Goal: more walk-ins, more Wolt orders, more event enquiries. Built by directing an AI coding assistant; I am not a developer, but I owned every architecture and content decision.

Weight: 7.4 MB deployed, of which 7.0 MB is images (14 photos + 48 menu thumbnails). `index.html` 766 lines, `styles.css` ~400, `script.js` 146.

## What I rejected, and why — tell me if I am wrong

**1. "The client-side sanitiser is pure security theatre — strip it out."**
The language toggle parses content inside an inert `<template>` and filters against tag/attribute allowlists before inserting it. The content is entirely author-written. I kept it: removing `innerHTML` assignment costs nothing to maintain, and re-adding the protection later is more expensive than leaving it. One reviewer called it theatre, the other called it acceptable defence in depth. **Who is right?**

**2. "46 custom Python CI checks are wasted effort for a small static site."**
I kept them. They are dependency-free, run in seconds, and one of them caught the documentation contradiction above that both human and AI review had missed. **Is this genuine value or am I rationalising sunk cost?**

**3. "Use a templating engine instead of `data-en` attributes + regex rewriting."**
This is the criticism I find hardest to dismiss (see open questions below).

## Current architecture, for review

**Bilingual.** Hungarian is the source of truth. Every translatable element carries a `data-en` attribute:

```html
<span class="mi-name" data-en="Beef stew">Marhapörkölt</span>
```

`scripts/build-en.py` (178 lines, regex-based) reads the Hungarian HTML, lifts each `data-en` value into the visible text, drops the attribute, rewrites relative asset paths to root-relative, swaps meta/OG/canonical to English, converts the language toggle into a link back to `/`, and rebuilds the FAQ JSON-LD from the translated questions. CI fails if the committed English page differs from what the generator produces.

Reciprocal `hreflang` (hu / en / x-default) plus self-referencing canonicals on both pages, mirrored in the sitemap.

**Privacy posture.** Fonts self-hosted (14 WOFF2 files). Google Maps replaced with a click-to-load placeholder. Analytics is Simple Analytics (cookieless). Measured on a fresh load: **zero cookies, zero iframes**; the only browser storage is `localStorage['kpt-lang']` holding the language choice, which I treat as ePrivacy Art. 5(3) strictly-necessary. Conclusion: no cookie banner required.

**Legal.** Imprint on every page (company name, seat, registration number, VAT number, email, phone). Bilingual privacy notice at `/adatvedelem/` and `/en/privacy/`, linked from the footer and directly under the form's submit button: controller identity, what the form collects, legal basis (Art. 6(1)(b) pre-contractual, falling back to 6(1)(f)), retention (1 year for enquiries, 8 years for accounting records), processors, data subject rights, NAIH for complaints. The form does not ask for allergies, and the notice asks people not to put health data in the free-text field.

**Headers.** CSP (`default-src 'self'`, with Simple Analytics allowed for script/img/connect and `frame-src https://www.google.com` for the post-click map), HSTS with `includeSubDomains`, `X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`, `Permissions-Policy` locking camera/mic/geolocation/payment.

**Quality gates.** 46 checks in CI (broken anchors, image existence and alt text, JSON-LD parsing, phone consistency, form integrity, hreflang reciprocity, English page freshness, repo-wide secret scan, sanitiser regression guard). Pre-commit hook blocking secrets and audit failures. Weekly live smoke test.

**Accessibility.** Contrast measured across the palette (worst case 5.9:1 against a 4.5 requirement), `:focus-visible` rings, intrinsic `width`/`height` on every image.

## Open questions for this round

**1. The generator.** Is regex-based HTML rewriting a time bomb I should replace before it bites, or acceptable for a page this size with a CI freshness check guarding it? If I should replace it: with what, given no npm and no build step at deploy? A Python templating engine run locally, with the output committed, is the obvious answer — but that means splitting content out of the HTML into data files, which makes the site harder for a non-developer to edit later. **Which trade-off is correct here?**

**2. Images — 7 MB.** I know WebP/AVIF plus `srcset` is the fix. Without a build pipeline, that means committing multiple derivatives of 62 images and hand-writing `srcset` attributes, or generating them with a local script (another generator, see question 1). **Is `<picture>` with committed WebP worth the repo bloat, or should I just re-compress the JPEGs harder and accept it?** What is the actual threshold where this stops mattering for a restaurant site?

**3. The health-data disclaimer.** The notice asks people not to enter allergies in the free-text field. One reviewer called this "legally convenient but commercially hostile" — a restaurant should *want* allergy information. They are right that I cannot un-receive it. **What is the correct design?** A separate consented field? Say nothing and handle it operationally? Route dietary needs to a phone call?

**4. Retention: 1 year.** I picked this figure; it is not derived from anything. **What would a defensible retention period for restaurant event enquiries actually be, and how should it be justified in the notice?**

**5. Reviews on the page.** Four Google reviews, quoted with the reviewer's name, plus a link to the listing and a line saying where they come from. I removed the API automation once the Maps terms turned out to forbid caching review text. **Is hand-copying meaningfully different, or should I drop the quotes entirely and show only the rating with a link?** The reviewer owns the copyright to their words either way.

**6. What is missing that I have not thought of at all?** This is the question I most want answered. Assume I have tunnel vision from working on this for weeks.

**7. Commercially.** Both previous reviewers said: stop polishing the repo, go do Google Business Profile work. I accept that. **Given that, is there anything left in the website itself worth doing before I stop?** Or is the site now "good enough" and every further hour better spent on Maps, Wolt and getting into Budapest restaurant listicles?
